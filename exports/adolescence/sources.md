# Scientific sources and teaching-model limits

Reviewed 2 November 2026. Narration is an original explanation for Grade 7 learners.

- NIH News in Health, [Navigating Puberty](https://newsinhealth.nih.gov/2026/08/navigating-puberty): hormones, growth, typical physical changes, wet dreams and differences in timing.
- Sydney Children's Hospitals Network, [Puberty in teenagers](https://www.schn.health.nsw.gov.au/kids-health-hub/growth-and-development/teenagers/puberty): common changes in girls and boys, gradual development, body-shape variation and voice changes.
- NHS-affiliated Your Sexual Health Matters, [Puberty and your body](https://www.yoursexualhealthmatters.org.uk/sexual-health-and-wellbeing/puberty-and-your-body): shared changes, puberty starting ranges, hygiene and professional support for worries.
- UNICEF Parenting, [Talking about periods at home](https://www.unicef.org/parenting/health/talking-about-periods-at-home): menstruation as a normal biological process, menstrual-product care, school participation and trusted support.
- NHS, [Early or delayed puberty](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/early-or-delayed-puberty/): variation in onset and seeking advice about development concerns.

## Design and model limits

- Clothed characters, skin cross-sections, the voice-box diagram and personal-care icons are original code-drawn teaching illustrations, not photographs or clinical examinations.
- Proportions and timing are illustrative. Changes are compressed into seconds, not presented as changes that happen in a day. The figures are not height predictions, ideal body shapes or clinical puberty stages.
- Illustrative “earlier” and “later” figures do not assign a fixed age, body shape, breast size or amount of facial hair to every learner.
- The puberty-onset bands of about 8–13 years for girls and 9–14 years for boys are broad common **starting** ranges, not completion deadlines or a diagnosis. The lesson directs concerns to a trusted adult or health worker.
- The hormone pathway is schematic, not a complete hypothalamus/pituitary/ovary/testis pathway. Other glands and hormones also contribute.
- The hair follicle, sweat gland and voice-box models are simplified and enlarged. They do not depict exact dimensions, vibration frequencies or individual anatomy.
- The menstrual scene shows the adult reproductive-organ layout to explain where the lining is shed; it is not a before/after image of a young person's genitals. Coloured moving markers symbolise blood and lining passing through the cervix and vagina.
- Early menstrual cycles can be irregular and do not always involve ovulation. Menstruation is explained without claiming that every early cycle includes release of an egg.
- Reproductive maturation does not imply emotional maturity, consent or readiness for pregnancy or parenting. The lesson concerns physical changes and self-care.
- No external copyrighted illustrations or stock photographs are included. The reproductive cutaway is reused from an earlier academy lesson generated with built-in ImageGen; its prompt is preserved with the asset.
