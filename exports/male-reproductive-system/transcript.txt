The male reproductive system
Welcome to CBE STEM Academy, Grade Seven Integrated Science. Today we learn about the male reproductive system. We will name its main parts, explain their jobs, and follow the route taken by sperm.

A map of the main parts
This side-view cutaway shows parts usually hidden inside the body. Some organs are inside the pelvis. Others are outside. Find the testis, scrotum, sperm duct, glands, penis, and urethra. The bladder helps us locate the organs. It belongs to the urinary system.

Testes: where sperm are made
There are usually two testes. One is called a testis. They make sperm, the male reproductive cells. Sperm production normally starts during puberty. Sperm develop inside tiny, coiled tubes. The testes also make testosterone. This hormone is a chemical messenger that helps control body changes.

Scrotum: a protective skin pouch
The scrotum is a skin pouch that holds the testes outside the main body cavity. Sperm production works best slightly below normal body temperature. Muscles move the testes closer to the body in cold conditions and farther away in warm conditions. This helps control temperature.

Epididymis: sperm mature here
Beside each testis is a tightly coiled tube called the epididymis. Sperm move from the testis into this tube. Here, sperm continue to mature and are stored for a time. Remember: the testis makes sperm. The epididymis helps them mature and stores them. The enlarged picture shows its coils.

Sperm duct: the transport tube
The sperm duct carries sperm away from the epididymis. Its other name is the vas deferens. Each testis has its own duct. This duct travels up into the pelvis and around the bladder. Muscles in its wall squeeze to move sperm along. Watch the dots show the direction.

Glands add fluid
Glands are small organs that make and release substances. The seminal vesicles lie behind the bladder. They add fluid containing sugar that supplies energy to sperm. The prostate is just below the bladder. It also adds fluid. Other small glands add a slippery fluid to the urethra. Sperm mixed with these fluids is called semen. Sperm are cells; semen is the mixture that carries them. The bladder stores urine, not semen.

Penis and urethra
The penis is an external organ. A tube called the urethra runs through it and opens at the tip. Urine and semen both leave through the urethra, but normally at different times. During ejaculation, a muscle at the bladder opening helps stop urine and semen from mixing.

Follow the sperm pathway
Follow the pathway. Sperm are made in the testes. They move into the epididymis to mature and be stored. Next, sperm travel along the sperm duct. Fluid from a seminal vesicle joins the pathway. A short joining tube passes through the prostate into the urethra. Glands add more fluid. Semen passes through the urethra and leaves at the tip of the penis. This release is called ejaculation. Sperm do not enter the bladder. Muscles help move them through the ducts. The dots are enlarged and the movement is speeded up.

A closer look at a sperm cell
A sperm cell is tiny. We need a microscope to see it clearly. Its head carries genetic information for inherited features. The middle piece releases energy. The tail helps it swim. During fertilisation, a sperm joins with an egg cell. We will explore that process in another lesson.

Puberty and healthy habits
During puberty, the organs develop and sperm production begins. A boy may notice a deeper voice and more body hair. People develop at different times. Keep the outside of the genital area clean. Ask a trusted adult or health worker about worrying changes. Never tease someone about their body.
