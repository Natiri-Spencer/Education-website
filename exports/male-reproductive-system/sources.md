# Scientific references

Checked 29 October 2026. The narration is original educational material. Anatomical artwork is adapted from BruceBlaus / Blausen Medical under CC BY-SA 4.0; see assets/ATTRIBUTION.md. No source prose is reproduced.

- [OpenStax: Anatomy and Physiology of the Male Reproductive System](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/27-1-anatomy-and-physiology-of-the-male-reproductive-system): testicular structure, epididymis, scrotal temperature control, sperm structure, testosterone and transport.
- [National Cancer Institute / NIH: Duct System](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/reproductive/male/duct.html): epididymis → vas deferens → ejaculatory duct → urethra.
- [National Cancer Institute / NIH: Accessory Glands](https://training.seer.cancer.gov/anatomy/reproductive/male/glands.html): seminal vesicles, prostate, bulbourethral glands, gland fluids and semen.
- [OpenStax: Human Reproductive Anatomy and Gametogenesis](https://openstax.org/books/biology/pages/43-3-human-reproductive-anatomy-and-gametogenesis): organ positions and reproductive cells.

## Teaching model limits

The main illustration is a detailed medical side-view pelvic cutaway, not a clinical scan or a view of a particular person. It displays the normal-anatomy panel of the licensed BruceBlaus source, with surrounding human tissues visible. One testis, epididymis, sperm duct and seminal vesicle are visible, while narration explains the paired organs. The bladder is included for orientation and is identified as a urinary organ. The short joining tube is the ejaculatory duct; the simpler phrase is used for Grade 7.

Gold markers show sperm transport; teal markers show added fluid or the urethral route. Markers are enlarged, motion is speeded up, and the drawing is not to scale. The bladder is outside the sperm pathway. Muscular transport through ducts is distinguished from the tail's role in swimming. The model does not simulate semen volume, sperm production rates or hormonal feedback.

Puberty timing varies. The lesson avoids fixed ages or suggesting that all learners develop at the same time. Fertilisation is introduced only briefly, for a later lesson.
