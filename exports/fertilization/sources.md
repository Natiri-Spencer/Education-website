# Scientific references and model limits

Checked 30 October 2026. The narration and animation are original educational material. No source illustration or source prose is reproduced.

- [OpenStax: Fertilization](https://openstax.org/books/anatomy-and-physiology/pages/28-1-fertilization): movement through the tract, loss of sperm, capacitation, egg coverings and protective changes after sperm entry.
- [NIH / NICHD: Conception infographic, text alternative](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/newsroom/digital-media/infographics/conception-textalt): fertilisation in the tube, followed by early development and movement towards the uterus.
- [Suarez & Wolfner, 2023: Sperm in the Mammalian Female Reproductive Tract: Surfing Through the Tract to Try to Beat the Odds](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37906840/): transport, tract barriers and the small fraction of sperm that reach the fertilisation site.
- [Alberts et al.: Fertilization, Molecular Biology of the Cell](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26843/): membrane fusion, the cortical reaction, pronuclei and combining genetic information.
- [NIH / NICHD: Menstruation factsheet](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/factsheets/menstruation): ovulation, the egg's route and implantation as a subsequent process.

## Accuracy choices

- Fertilisation is placed in the wider fallopian tube (ampulla), rather than in the uterus or ovary.
- The route starts with sperm in the vagina, then passes through the cervix and uterus into a tube. Sexual activity is not depicted.
- Tail motion and tract movement both contribute to transport; sperm are not shown navigating as conscious agents.
- The terms egg and ovum are used at a Grade 7 level. The ovulated human cell is more precisely a secondary oocyte; its detailed meiotic stages are beyond this lesson.
- The corona/cumulus cells, zona pellucida and cell membrane are distinct in the close-up. Enzymes and sperm preparation are introduced without claiming that every molecular event follows a single universally fixed timing.
- Exactly one highlighted sperm enters. Its tail also moves inside and fades to indicate later breakdown.
- Entry triggers protective coat and membrane changes. The effect is biological, not an electrical force field. Extra sperm remain outside and do not contribute genetic information to that zygote.
- Loss with fluid, mucus trapping, defence-cell clearing and breakdown are described as different fates. Sperm do not all die at once or only because one has entered an egg. A separate labelled inset illustrates defence-cell clearing; it is an example, not the fate of every sperm.
- Two pronuclei are shown within one cell. They are not depicted directly fusing their nuclear membranes. The narration says genetic information comes from both parents.
- Two- and four-cell division views are simplified teaching models. Implantation is identified as a later process, not fertilisation.

## Visual limits

Cell sizes and marker counts are simplified. Colours distinguish tracking and genetic contributions rather than literal colours under a microscope. Events are time-compressed; the five-minute video is not a real-time recording. The cortical changes and clearance are illustrative rather than a complete molecular or immune simulation. No fixed number of sperm, survival time or fastest-sperm rule is claimed.
