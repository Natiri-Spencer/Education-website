Keep a green plant in darkness for forty-eight hours. Photosynthesis stops, while respiration uses stored food. This reduces existing starch before our test.
Cover the middle of a leaf on both sides with black paper and clips.
Place the plant in sunlight for several hours. Only exposed areas receive light.
Detach the leaf with forceps. Remove the paper and note its position.
Immerse the leaf in hot water. This kills the tissue, stops chemical reactions, and helps testing solutions enter.
Warm the leaf in ethanol, using a hot-water bath. Ethanol removes green chlorophyll so iodine colours are visible. Heat indirectly because ethanol is flammable; never use a naked flame.
Rinse in warm water to soften the leaf. Spread it on a white tile.
Add iodine. Exposed areas turn blue-black: starch is present. The covered strip stays yellow-brown: no starch is detected. This comparison shows that light is needed for starch production.
