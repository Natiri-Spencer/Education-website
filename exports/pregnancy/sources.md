# Scientific sources and artwork

Reviewed 1 November 2026. Narration is an original Grade 7 explanation.

- NICHD / NIH, [How does the placenta form?](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/research/supported/human-placenta-project/how-does-placenta-form): implantation, placental development and exchange.
- NICHD / NIH, [Researchers discover how embryo attaches to the uterus](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/news/releases/Pages/embryo.aspx): blastocyst attachment around six days after fertilisation.
- UCSF Center for Reproductive Health, [How conception works](https://crh.ucsf.edu/about-fertility/conception/): division, transport, blastocyst formation and hatching.
- NHS, [Week 4](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/) and [Week 10](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-10/): early development and embryo/fetus terminology.
- U.S. Office on Women's Health, [Stages of pregnancy](https://womenshealth.gov/pregnancy/youre-pregnant-now-what/stages-pregnancy): developmental milestones and trimesters.
- NICHD / NIH, [Pregnancy](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pregnancy): about 40 weeks from the last menstrual period.

## Artwork

The finished lesson uses original generated medical illustrations, with built-in ImageGen prompts documented in `assets/visual-prompts.md`. The female reproductive cutaway is reused from the academy's earlier lesson. All are educational illustrations, not clinical scans.

Office on Women's Health images were consulted as references during preparation. Their visible third-party copyright notices mean they are not treated as unrestricted public-domain assets; they are excluded from the finished lesson and handoff pack.

## Teaching-model limits

- Normal human development is shown; implantation outside the uterus is not modelled.
- Cells, organ views and fetal close-ups use different scales. Months of growth are compressed into five minutes.
- The early cell group divides without the large increase in total size suggested by enlarged cell markers.
- Implantation begins around 6–7 days after fertilisation and proceeds over several days. The microscopic cutaway illustrates attachment, not every intermediate cellular step.
- The placenta and fetal membranes develop over time. The explanation of the placenta and cord uses a labelled late-pregnancy organ view, rather than implying they are already fully formed immediately after implantation.
- Coloured moving dots indicate transport directions. They do not imply direct mixing of maternal and fetal blood.
- The embryo/fetus transition is described as about eight weeks after fertilisation, roughly ten pregnancy weeks. The OWH page's eight-week terminology differs; the narration follows the NHS terminology.
- Trimester transitions and developmental illustrations are approximate. Timing and delivery dates vary; a due date is an estimate.
