Acids, Bases, and Indicators

INTEGRATED SCIENCE

Introduction

Welcome to the CEMASTEA Virtual Lab on Acids, Bases, and Indicators. In this lesson, you will explore household solutions and use a hibiscus indicator to investigate how acids, bases, and neutral solutions can be identified by colour change. Begin with the pre-assessment, then follow the guided experiment and record your observations.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

  • Describe how an indicator helps identify acids, bases, and neutral solutions.
  • Prepare a natural indicator from hibiscus petals.
  • Use colour observations to classify sample solutions and record evidence.

Resources

The simulation provides a mortar and pestle, hibiscus petals, distilled water, a filter funnel, a beaker, an indicator dropper, and three sample solutions.

Safety: This is a simulation. Do not taste or handle real household chemicals. Ask your teacher before doing any practical activity.

Pre-Assessment

Think about these questions before starting. You can return to them after the experiment.

  1. What is an indicator used for?
  2. What colour change might help you identify an acid or a base?

Procedure & Experiment

⚗

INTEGRATED SCIENCE | ACIDS, BASES, AND INDICATORS

Grade 7 · Acids, Bases and Indicators

Prepare a hibiscus indicator, add it to lemon juice, soapy water, and plain water, then use the colour changes to classify each sample.

Post-Assessment

After completing the experiment, check your understanding:

  1. Which sample behaved as an acid, and what colour did the indicator show?
  2. Which colour change helped you identify a base?
  3. Why should you record the observed colour before classifying a sample?

Conclusion

Indicators provide evidence about the properties of a solution through colour change. Compare all three observations and explain how each colour supported your classification.

PROCEDURE

  1. 1Place the mortar on the workbench.
  2. 2Add hibiscus petals to the mortar.
  3. 3Grind the petals with the pestle.
  4. 4Add distilled water to extract the pigment.
  5. 5Place the empty beaker on the workbench.
  6. 6Place the filter funnel over the beaker to strain the indicator.
  7. 7Select a household solution to test.
  8. 8Add a few drops of indicator to the sample.
  9. 9Observe the colour and classify the solution.
Start by choosing the mortar from the apparatus shelf.

Simulation only: do not taste or handle real substances. Follow your teacher’s safety instructions during any practical activity.
