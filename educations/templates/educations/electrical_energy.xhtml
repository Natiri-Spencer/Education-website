PROCEDURE

    Series bulbs share one path. Parallel bulbs need separate paths that rejoin. The switch should control every bulb.

    The ammeter goes in the route carrying the current you chose to measure. The voltmeter bridges the two ends of the chosen source or bulb. Check red (+) and black (−) meter leads.

    Virtual dry cells only. Never connect a real ammeter directly across a cell or short a cell. Real parallel cells must be matched.

      About this model

      You choose apparatus positions and wiring. Choosing a challenge does not build its connections. The checker accepts electrically equivalent arrangements. Wires join only at terminals; a crossing does not make a junction. Lamp-holder terminals 1 and 2 are not polarised. The drawings represent modern school equipment: digital meters, modular switches, cell holders and lamp holders. Exact models vary.

      Each ideal dry cell supplies 1.50 V. Identical bulbs have a fixed resistance of 10 Ω. Wires and the ammeter have zero resistance; the voltmeter draws no current. Brightness follows bulb power, relative to a bulb supplied with 3 V. Real bulbs have changing resistance, and real cells have internal resistance and limited capacity.

      Matching cells in parallel supply the voltage of one cell. They can supply energy for longer; this simulation does not model discharge time.
